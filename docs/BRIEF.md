# 7 WELLS INDIES — Creative Brief

## What the Eye4U reference actually does (observed, not assumed)

Captured live via Ruffle at `ref/shots/`. What makes it compelling:

1. **The screen is a stage, not a document.** Fixed viewport, letterboxed, zero scrolling.
   Nothing is "below the fold" because there is no fold.
2. **Enormous flat vector shapes in motion.** Starburst wedges, a teal circle swallowing
   the frame, a black diagonal wipe. Shapes are bigger than the screen and move through it.
3. **Hard-cut rhythm.** The intro is a performance edited on beats, not a page that loads.
4. **Severely limited palette per moment.** Orange/red/white, then teal/orange/white.
   Each scene commits to 3 colors.
5. **The logo is a character.** The eye blinks and winks. Identity behaves, not just sits.
6. **Navigation is a diagram, not a menu.** A photographic eye *looks at* an arc of
   green→cyan beads. Three labels in heavy condensed italic. The nav is an image with
   meaning, and the gaze tells you where to look.
7. **Confident contrast of scale.** A tiny 9px "skip" against a screen-filling starburst.
8. **Few doors.** Three sections. Scarcity makes each one feel significant.

**What we take:** stage-not-document, giant flat shapes, cut-on-beat, committed palettes,
identity-as-character, nav-as-diagram, scale contrast, few doors.
**What we do NOT take:** the eye, orange-on-white, the bead arc, Futura Extra Black Italic,
the starburst. Those are Eye4U's. We need our own.

## The experience

> **A dark desert field seen from above at night. Seven holes are punched into the ground.
> You carry a light. You lean over a well, and something is down there. You drop in.**

- **The Surface** is the hub: a night-desert plane, seven wells, your cursor is a light
  source. Hovering a well wakes it — it breathes, it leaks a sound, it shows a sliver of
  what lives below. There is no menu bar. The field IS the menu.
- **Descending** is a real vertical fall, not a page transition. The camera drops down a
  shaft. This is the "cut on a beat" moment.
- **Each chamber** is a different room with its own palette and its own behaviour, built
  around one real channel. A chamber is not a landing page — it is a place.
- **The Seventh Well** is unlabeled, appears dry, and is the discovery payload. It should
  reward the curious with something genuinely strange and game-like.

**Mystery rule:** nothing explains itself up front. No "Welcome to 7 Wells Indies" banner,
no hero paragraph, no "Our Community" section. Meaning accumulates through exploration.
Usability comes from *legible affordances* (things that obviously want to be touched),
not from instructions.

## Visual language

Night desert, chalk survey marks, phosphor water.

```
--night   #0B0E1A   deep indigo-black — the ground at night
--night-2 #141A2E   raised ground
--deep    #050710   the void inside a well
--bone    #F2EDE2   chalk / bone / survey line
--sand    #E8873A   hot sand, lamp light, heat
--rust    #B8341F   dried clay, danger, the old
--water   #3BE8B0   phosphor green-cyan — the signal of water, life, a running process
```

Commit to **3 colors per scene**. The hub is night + bone + sand. Water-green is the
*reward* color: it only appears when something is alive or found.

- **Type:** `Anton` (ultra-condensed heavy display — our answer to Futura XBlkCnIt without
  being it), `IBM Plex Mono` (survey data, coordinates, labels), `Heebo` (Hebrew).
  Bilingual Hebrew/Latin is an authentic 7 Wells detail — use it, don't ornament with it.
- **Texture:** film grain, halftone dot, chalk-line imperfection. Hand-surveyed, not
  vector-perfect. This is a desert field someone mapped by hand.
- **Motion:** heavy, physical, with weight. Things fall. Dust settles. Water ripples.
  Nothing eases politely.
- **Sound:** synthesized in WebAudio, no asset files. Low drone, stone, drips, a rising
  tone on descent. Muted by default with an obvious, charming unmute.

---

## Bar for "done"

A visitor who has never heard of 7 Wells should, within 90 seconds, have: touched
something that reacted in a way they didn't expect, descended into at least two wells,
discovered that the seventh is different, and understood without being told that this is
a desert game-dev community in Be'er Sheva. They should want to show someone else.
